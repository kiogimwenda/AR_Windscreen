4.0 Expected Results

The expected results are set out per specific objective. Because the software was developed ahead of the hardware, several results have already been measured on recorded data and in simulation; these are reported as achieved to date, and the remaining results will be measured on the hardware and in the vehicle.

4.1 Objective 1: Perception and Sensor Fusion

A perception pipeline that processes every frame of 2K video in real time on the laptop GPU, producing for each frame: instance masks and classes for vehicles, pedestrians, cyclists and obstacles; Kenyan road signs; lane lines; and a relative depth map ({{ref:perception_krakow_lanes.jpg}} and {{ref:sign_speed_hump_kenya.jpg}}, Appendix F). With the LiDAR installed, each detected object will carry a measured range, and tracks will carry velocity, predicted path and collision probability.

4.2 Objective 2: Road-Locked AR Navigation

An AR display in which the route appears painted on the road in the car’s lane, following slopes and dips within LiDAR range and fading honestly to a flat-road estimate beyond it; offline turn-by-turn routing anywhere within 100 km of Nairobi, with fast rerouting around closures; and sign, hazard and turn overlays that never hide a real hazard. The road-projection check (Section 3.10) is expected to show the route line inside the correct lane within LiDAR range.

4.3 Objective 3: Hazard Assessment and Fail-Safe Actuation

Hazard and reckless-driving warnings shown as colour-graded glows and zones; automatic hazard lights under hard braking; and a brake actuation that engages only at a measured TTC below 1.8 s, is always overridable by the driver’s foot, is cut instantly by the emergency stop, and releases within 200 ms of losing the laptop, with every event recorded in the evidence log. The bench rig will produce force, current and response-time curves across intensities, and video of the override, kill-switch and unplug tests.

5.0 Project Time Plan

{{ref:fig_gantt.svg}} shows the project schedule. The software-only phases are complete. The critical path now runs through procurement: the LiDAR, the BNO085 IMU and the actuator are not stocked locally and will be imported in a single consignment, so their lead time (including customs clearance) governs when LiDAR integration and the bench gate can start. Work that does not depend on the imports (hub bring-up with local parts, camera calibration, the bench rig itself) proceeds in parallel. The custom PCBs (Phase 12B) are designed while the bench gate runs on module hardware, and must then pass the same gate again.
